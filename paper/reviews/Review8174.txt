Reviewer 1 of OCEANS 2026 submission 120

Comments to the author
======================

The abstract addresses a relevant and important problem for
marine control systems: identifying dynamic models of small
underactuated USVs from limited field data for subsequent
model-based control and observer design. The proposed
comparison across interpretable first-order models,
structured nonlinear models, and black-box NARX models is
potentially useful, especially in the context of balancing
control-oriented simplicity with predictive accuracy.

However, the contribution is not yet clearly formulated. In
particular:
- The abstract states that the models are used
“collectively,” but the actual methodology for combining or
comparing them is not explained.
- It is unclear what decision process connects the three
model levels to controller or observer design, and whether
the output is intended to be a modeling workflow, a
diagnostic tool, or a specific control-oriented
model-selection method.

The evaluation also needs clarification. In particular:
- The reported	values should be explicitly defined,
including whether they are computed on training or
validation data, and whether they correspond to one-step
prediction or free-run simulation.
- The statement that the NARX model achieves high one-step
prediction accuracy but diverges in closed-loop simulation
is potentially interesting, but the closed-loop setup,
controller, simulation horizon, and mechanism of divergence
are not described. Without this information, it is
difficult to understand the meaning of the result.

The abstract also lacks any reference to literature
pointing to Nomoto-style models, USV system identification,
control-oriented modeling, and nonlinear/black-box system
identification. The lack of literature makes difficult to
understand the novelty relative to prior USV identification
studies is not clear. This should also be discussed in the
introduction.

Overall, the topic is relevant and the preliminary results
are promising, but the abstract lacks sufficient
methodological detail, literature context, and clarity of
contribution.
